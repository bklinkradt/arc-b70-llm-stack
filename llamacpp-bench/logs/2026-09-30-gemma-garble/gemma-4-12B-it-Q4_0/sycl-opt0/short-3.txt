The French Revolution was caused by a complex convergence of social, political, economic, and intellectual factors that rendered the *Ancien Régime* unsustainable.

**Social Inequality** was a primary driver. France was divided into three estates: the Clergy, the Nobility, and the Commoners (Third Estate). While the first two enjoyed immense privileges and tax exemptions, the Third Estate—comprising 98% of the population—bore the entire tax burden while possessing little political power. This sparked deep-seated resentment among the peasantry and the rising bourgeoisie.

**Economic Crisis** exacerbated these tensions. Years of costly wars, including the American Revolution, left the monarchy bankrupt. A series of bad harvests led to widespread famine and skyrocketing bread prices, pushing the lower classes toward desperation.

**Enlightenment Ideals** provided the intellectual framework for revolt. Philosophers like Rousseau and Voltaire championed concepts of liberty, equality, and popular sovereignty, inspiring the bourgeoisie to challenge the "Divine Right" of kings.

Finally, **Weak Leadership** played a crucial role. King Louis XVI’s indecisiveness and Marie Antoinette’s perceived extravagance alienated the public, creating a vacuum of authority that eventually collapsed into revolution.
