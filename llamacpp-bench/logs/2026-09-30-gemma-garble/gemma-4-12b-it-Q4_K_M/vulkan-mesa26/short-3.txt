The French Revolution was caused by a complex convergence of social, economic, and political crises that destabilized the Bourbon monarchy.

**Social Inequality:** The "Ancien Régime" divided society into three estates. The First (Clergy) and Second (Nobility) Estates enjoyed immense privileges and tax exemptions, while the Third Estate (peasants, city workers, and bourgeoisie) bore the entire tax burden despite having the least wealth. This systemic inequality fueled deep-seated resentment against the aristocracy.

**Economic Crisis:** France faced a severe financial collapse due to extravagant royal spending and costly involvement in foreign wars, such as the American Revolution. This was exacerbated by a series of poor harvests, leading to skyrocketing bread prices and widespread famine. The starving populace became increasingly radicalized against the monarchy’s perceived indifference.

**Enlightenment Ideals:** Intellectual shifts played a crucial role. Philosophers like Rousseau and Voltaire challenged the "Divine Right of Kings," advocating for liberty, equality, and democratic governance. These ideas provided the ideological framework for the Third Estate to demand a constitution and a voice in government.

Ultimately, the combination of an empty treasury, a starving population, and revolutionary ideas made the monarchy's collapse inevitable.
