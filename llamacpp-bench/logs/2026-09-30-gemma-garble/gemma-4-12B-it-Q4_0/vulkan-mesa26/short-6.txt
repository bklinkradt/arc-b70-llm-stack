### Translation
**"Das Wetter war schlecht, deshalb blieben wir zu Hause und lasen Bücher."**

*(Alternative common version: "Das Wetter war schlecht, also blieben wir zu Hause und haben Bücher gelesen.")*

---

### Grammar Explanations

#### 1. Word Order with "deshalb" (Conjunctional Adverbs)
In the sentence, **"deshalb"** means "therefore" or "that's why." Unlike some English conjunctions (like "so"), *deshalb* is a **conjunctival adverb**. 

In German, these types of adverbs occupy **Position 1** in the main clause. Because the verb must always be in **Position 2** (the V2 rule), the subject must move to the position immediately following the verb.
*   **Structure:** [Cause] + **deshalb** (Pos 1) + **blieben** (Verb/Pos 2) + **wir** (Subject) + [Rest of sentence].
*   If you used the word **"so"** instead, the word order would stay the same as English: *"Das Wetter war schlecht, **so blieben** wir..."* (However, *deshalb* is much more common in this context).

#### 2. Präteritum vs. Perfekt (Past Tenses)
In the translation provided, I used the **Präteritum** (Simple Past) tense: *blieben* (stayed) and *lasen* (read). 

*   **Präteritum:** This is the standard tense for written stories, reports, and formal narratives. It is the "literary" past.
*   **Perfekt:** In spoken German, people almost always use the Perfekt tense (*wir sind zu Hause geblieben und haben Bücher gelesen*). 

Because your original sentence is a simple narrative statement, both are correct, but *Präteritum* gives it a slightly more polished, "story-like" feel, while *Perfekt* would be what you would say to a friend in a conversation.
