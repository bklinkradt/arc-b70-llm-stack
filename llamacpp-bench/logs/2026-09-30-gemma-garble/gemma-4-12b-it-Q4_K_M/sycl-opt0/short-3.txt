The French Revolution was caused by a complex convergence of social, economic, and political crises that destabilized the Bourbon monarchy.

**Social Inequality:** The "Ancien Régime" was defined by a rigid class structure. The First Estate (clergy) and Second Estate (nobility) enjoyed immense privileges and tax exemptions, while the Third Estate (peasants, urban workers, and bourgeoisie) bore the entire tax burden despite having little political power. This fueled deep-seated resentment against aristocratic privilege.

**Economic Distress:** By the late 1780s, France faced a severe financial crisis. Years of costly wars—including involvement in the American Revolution—coupled with extravagant royal spending, left the treasury bankrupt. Simultaneously, a series of bad harvests led to skyrocketing bread prices and widespread famine, pushing the peasantry to the brink of starvation.

**Enlightenment Ideals:** Intellectual shifts played a crucial role. Philosophers like Rousseau and Voltaire challenged the "Divine Right of Kings," advocating for liberty, equality, and democratic governance. These ideas provided the ideological framework for the bourgeoisie to demand a constitutional government.

Ultimately, the combination of an empty treasury, a starving populace, and a revolutionary intellectual climate made the collapse of the monarchy inevitable.
