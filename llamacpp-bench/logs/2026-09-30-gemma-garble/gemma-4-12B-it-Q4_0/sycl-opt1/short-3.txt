The French Revolution was caused by a complex convergence of social, political, economic, and intellectual factors that rendered the existing "Ancien Régime" unsustainable.

**Social Inequality** was a primary driver. France was divided into three estates: the Clergy, the Nobility, and the Commoners (Third Estate). While the first two enjoyed immense privileges and tax exemptions, the Third Estate—comprising 98% of the population—bore the entire tax burden while having no political voice.

**Economic Distress** exacerbated these tensions. France was nearly bankrupt due to costly wars, including the American Revolution, and extravagant royal spending. A series of bad harvests led to widespread famine and skyrocketing bread prices, pushing the peasantry and urban poor to the brink of starvation.

**Enlightenment Ideals** provided the intellectual framework for revolt. Philosophers like Rousseau and Voltaire challenged the "Divine Right of Kings," advocating for liberty, equality, and representative government.

Finally, **Weak Leadership** played a crucial role. King Louis XVI was indecisive and unable to implement necessary reforms to curb the nobility's power, creating a political vacuum that eventually exploded into revolution in 1789.
