The French Revolution was caused by a complex convergence of social, economic, and political crises that destabilized the Bourbon monarchy.

**Social Inequality:** The "Ancien Régime" was defined by a rigid class structure. The First Estate (clergy) and Second Estate (nobility) enjoyed immense privileges and tax exemptions, while the Third Estate (peasants and bourgeoisie) bore the entire tax burden despite having little political power. This systemic inequality fueled deep-seated resentment among the common people.

**Economic Crisis:** France faced a severe financial collapse due to extravagant royal spending and costly involvement in foreign wars, such as the American Revolution. This was exacerbated by a series of poor harvests, leading to skyrocketing bread prices and widespread famine. By the late 1780s, the peasantry and urban poor were starving, creating a volatile atmosphere of desperation.

**Intellectual Shifts:** The Enlightenment provided the ideological framework for revolt. Philosophers like Rousseau and Voltaire challenged the "Divine Right of Kings," advocating for reason, liberty, and equality. These ideas inspired the bourgeoisie to demand a constitutional government and popular sovereignty.

Ultimately, these pressures forced King Louis XVI to convene the Estates-General, providing the spark that ignited the revolution.
